Approach

How I work

  1. Step 1

    Start with the workflow, not the model

    The first question is never which architecture. It's what someone is doing by hand right now, how often it goes wrong, and what it costs when it does. Half the time the answer isn't a model at all.

  2. Step 2

    Build to the constraint that actually binds

    Every deployment has one limit that decides the design. A budget, a latency ceiling, an audit requirement, a team that won't adopt another dashboard. Find it first and the architecture follows.

  3. Step 3

    Make it explain itself

    A score nobody can interrogate doesn't get used. Whoever has to act on the output, whether a regulator, an analyst or an engineer on call, needs to see why in language they already speak.

I got here by accident. What started as automating a boring spreadsheet task turned into building pipelines and training models, and four internships later, across network outages at MTN, root cause analysis at Huawei and delivery pipelines at Prunedge, the pattern was the same every time. The model was rarely the hard part. Getting it into the place where the work happens always was.
